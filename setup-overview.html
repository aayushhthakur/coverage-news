Pipeline (every 15 minutes)

  1. Ingest

    39 RSS feeds plus one GDELT DOC 2.0 query. Every raw response is saved as JSON.

    app/ingest.py → data/raw/YYYY-MM-DD/
  2. Normalise

    Title, summary of at most 280 characters (never full text), publisher, time, URL. Drops duplicates by URL and by normalised-title hash.

    app/normalise.py
  3. Classify

    Multi-label topic and subtopic by embedding similarity to the taxonomy descriptions, plus a small prior for the publisher's own topics.

    app/classify.py · MiniLM-L6-v2
  4. Cluster

    Joins the most similar open event if similarity is at least 0.75, within 48 hours. Event IDs stay the same between runs.

    app/cluster.py
  5. Group stories

    Events whose average vectors are at least 0.65 similar are linked. Groups of 6 or more become one story with sub-events.

    app/stories.py
  6. Snapshot + score

    Stores each event's publisher count every run, then computes C, V, T and S per topic.

    app/scoring.py
  7. Representative

    The article from the highest-tier publisher whose headline isn't clickbait, earliest first.

    app/rank.py
  8. Re-rank

    At request time: one entry per event, at most 2 articles per publisher, breaking boost, at most 3 per topic on Home.

    app/rank.py · app/main.py

Scoring

S = (0.4·C + 0.3·V + 0.3·T) × 2−a/h
TermDefinitionNotes
Clog(1 + P) / log(1 + N)P = distinct publishers in the event, N = 39 publishers in feeds.yaml
Vpublishers added in the last hour / P0 if P < 3. "An hour ago" comes from snapshots, or from article times on a fresh DB.
TTier 1 publishers covering it / Tier 1 list sizeTier 1 lists come from the tier field in feeds.yaml, per topic
a, hhours since latest article; topic half-lifeSports 6h · Technology 18h · Geopolitics 24h · Economics 36h
×0.5clickbait demotionApplied if every headline in the event is flagged (question bait, withheld subject, punctuation, capitals)

Publishers are counted, never articles. Stories are scored over all their sub-events' publishers combined. Every weight, half-life and threshold is in config/scoring.yaml.

Re-ranking rules

  • One entry per event, and a story replaces its member events.
  • At most 2 representative articles per publisher in any top 10. The event switches to its next-best publisher before it is dropped.
  • Breaking boost: under 2 hours old, with V above 0 and in the topic's top 5%. It moves into the topic's top 3 with a "Breaking" tag.
  • Home interleave: no topic takes more than 3 of the top 10.

API

EndpointReturns
GET /homeTop 10 across topics. Each card has headline, summary, publisher, url, first report time, sources, breaking flag and full score breakdown.
GET /topics/{topic}?subtopic=Top 10 for a topic (tech works, any case) plus its subtopic list. Unknown topic or subtopic returns 404.
GET /events/{id}Publishers grouped by tier (1/2/3/other), timeline of every report, score breakdown (C, V, T, age, decay, S).
GET /stories/{id}The same for a story, plus its sub-events ordered by score.
GET /healthLast run time, running flag, article/event/story counts, failed feeds.

Configuration and secrets

FileWhat it controls
config/feeds.yaml39 feeds: name (the publisher), url, topics, tier, and GDELT domains. Rebuild it with scripts/build_feeds.py.
config/taxonomy.yaml4 topics × 4 subtopics with descriptions; labelling thresholds; publisher prior.
config/scoring.yamlWeights, half-lives, cluster threshold (0.75) and window (48h), story grouping (0.65, 6+ events), breaking rule, re-rank caps, schedule (15 min), GDELT.
.envBackend-only secrets, loaded at startup. UNSPLASH_ACCESS_KEY is empty and nothing uses it yet. Never committed (in .gitignore); .env.example lists the keys.
Keep API keys in .env, not in a frontend VITE_… variable. Anything with a VITE_ name is bundled into the JavaScript the browser downloads, so anyone using the app could read it.

Known limitations

  • open GDELT returns HTTP 429 (or an empty list) from this network. It is reported in /health, and the app runs on RSS alone.
  • open Shopping posts: Wired's coupon and deal posts are not news events, but they are not clickbait by the spec's rules, so they can rank (#9 on Home now).
  • open Classification noise: "trade deadline" is labelled Economics and a rugby TV listing is labelled Cricket. About 20% of articles match no topic.
  • open Economics is thin: few feeds, so most Economics events have a single publisher.
  • note Velocity needs history: V is mostly 0 until snapshots an hour apart have built up.
  • note Story labels are the most frequent name in the headlines ("Giants"), not a written title.
